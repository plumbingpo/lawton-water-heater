Keeping Your Duncan Water Heater Running Longer

Many premature tank failures trace back to skipped maintenance, and the habits that prevent them are simple. Flush the tank once a year to clear sediment, get the anode rod checked every few years, and test the relief valve annually so it doesn't seize. Set the thermostat close to 120 degrees for safer water and less scale. If you have a tankless unit, descale it yearly where the water is hard to protect the heat exchanger.

The age of your heater matters just as much. With regular service, a tank water heater typically lasts 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts acting up, like rust-colored water, popping noises, or moisture around the base, it's time to plan a replacement. A planned replacement is cheaper and is far less stressful than an emergency swap with water spreading across the floor.

The sooner you call about a hot water problem, the smaller the repair tends to be. A sediment layer that's just starting to rumble needs only a flush, yet the same sediment, ignored for a few years, can overheat the bottom of the tank and shorten the life of the tank, forcing a replacement much sooner. Save our number in your contacts today so you can reach us fast the moment something seems off. A quick call saves time, money, and ruined floors.

Common Water Heater Problems in Duncan

A water heater in Duncan almost never fails out of nowhere. Pay attention to a popping or rumbling sound during heating, rust-colored hot water, a sulfur smell from the hot tap, moisture around the base, or hot water that runs out a little sooner each month. Those point to heavy sediment, a depleted anode, a weak element or burner, and early corrosion, and when they're caught early, most of them mean a modest repair or a replacement on your schedule. Left alone, they end in a failed tank and water on the floor. Pay close attention to a relief valve that drips or discharges: it means the tank is seeing too much pressure or temperature, and it should be checked promptly. We find the root cause instead of treating symptoms, so the small problem never gets the chance to grow.
