A tankless unit rethinks the whole idea of a water heater. There is no tank to keep hot, so no energy is spent reheating stored water while you sleep or travel. When a faucet or shower opens, the unit senses flow and fires, heating water on demand through a heat exchanger. The result is a continuous supply as long as demand stays within the unit's capacity, a compact footprint on the wall, and a lifespan that can be nearly double that of a typical tank. Homeowners in Lawton, OK often choose tankless during a replacement or remodel for exactly those reasons.

Sizing is where tankless installs succeed or fail. We size by flow rate and temperature rise: add up the fixtures you realistically run at the same time in gallons per minute, then factor in how cold the incoming water gets in winter, since a unit that delivers five gallons per minute at a mild rise may deliver far less when the groundwater is cold. Then comes the infrastructure. A whole-home gas unit can draw several times the gas of a standard tank, which often means a larger gas line or confirming the meter can keep up. Venting must match the unit, and electric tankless models can need a large share of your electrical service, sometimes enough to require a panel upgrade.

Every tankless install from Lawton Water Heater includes isolation service valves so future descaling takes minutes instead of hours, proper gas piping with a drip leg, correctly pitched and terminated venting, and a pressure relief valve piped to a safe location. If your water is hard, we talk through scale protection, since mineral buildup is what shortens tankless life most. We can add recirculation for faster hot water at distant bathrooms, set the temperature, handle permits where required, and walk you through maintenance before we leave. A heater that is starting to struggle rarely hides it for long, and the clues are easy to spot once you know them. If any of the following sound familiar, it's worth having the unit checked:

  • Construction or remodeling that opens walls and simplifies the install
  • Hot water running out during back-to-back showers
  • A utility area where a bulky tank takes up room you would rather use
  • Interest in a longer-lasting heater with replaceable parts
  • A distant bath or detached space that needs its own hot water source
  • Energy bills that reflect a tank heating water around the clock

Why This Job Calls for a Pro

Tankless installation is more involved than swapping a tank. A whole-home gas unit can demand several times the gas flow of a standard tank, and running it on an undersized line causes ignition faults and inconsistent temperatures. Venting is equally specific: condensing units use plastic vent pipe and need a condensate drain, non-condensing units need stainless steel, and neither can simply reuse the old tank's flue in most cases. Electric tankless can require several dedicated high-amperage breakers, and mistakes with gas, venting, or high-amperage wiring carry serious safety consequences.

Our technicians handle the full picture. We size the unit by flow rate and winter temperature rise instead of guessing, confirm gas pressure with the burner at maximum, and install venting and condensate lines exactly to specification. Every install gets isolation valves for easy descaling and a properly piped relief valve. We also give honest advice when tankless is not the best fit. For some households, a high-efficiency tank or a heat pump water heater makes more sense, and you will hear that from us before you spend money on a system that does not match how you live.

How to Prevent Water Heater Problems

A few preventive steps can help you avoid a cold shower or a wet floor down the road. Put these technician-approved steps to work at home:

  • Avoid cranking the temperature higher than you need; about 120F works for most homes
  • Check the vent cap outside for nests, debris, or buildup
  • Keep the inlet screen clear so flow stays strong and the unit fires reliably
  • Plan on yearly descaling through the service valves, sooner if your water is hard
  • Treating hard water protects the heat exchanger from mineral buildup
  • If the unit shows a recurring code, note it and have it checked
  • In freezing weather, keep the unit powered and insulate exposed piping
