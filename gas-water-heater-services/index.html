Most gas water heaters in Lawton, OK work the same basic way. Gas enters through a control valve that doubles as the thermostat and feeds a burner beneath the tank, and the hot exhaust travels up a flue through the middle of the tank and out through the vent. A thermocouple, or a flame sensor on newer electronic models, tells the valve a flame is present; without that signal, the valve closes for safety. The design is dependable, but it has a few predictable weak spots. Thermocouples wear out, pilot tubes and burner ports clog, the flame arrestor screen at the base packs up with lint and dust, control valves fail, and vents lose their draft.

Every gas water heater call starts with safety and then moves to testing. If you ever smell gas, leave the home first, call the gas utility from outside, and do not operate switches or try to relight anything. Once the area is safe, our Lawton technicians work through the heater systematically: millivolt readings on the thermocouple, a close look at the pilot and main burner flames, gas pressure at the control valve, a draft test to make sure exhaust is not spilling back, and a CO check around the appliance. Scorch marks at the burner access, rust flakes on the burner, and a lazy yellow flame each point to a different problem, and we chase down the right one.

The fix is usually straightforward once the cause is known. A new thermocouple or flame sensor, a cleaned pilot tube and burner, a cleared flame arrestor screen, or a replacement gas control valve gets most heaters running again the same day. Vent problems, like a disconnected connector or a flue pipe without enough rise, are corrected on the spot. If sediment is rumbling in the tank, we flush it, and if the anode rod is spent, we can replace it. When replacement is the smarter move, we install new gas heaters sized to your household's first-hour demand, with proper atmospheric, power vent, or direct vent venting, a drip leg on the gas supply, and the expansion tank, drain pan, and strapping your local code calls for. How to Tell Something Is Wrong

A heater that is starting to struggle rarely hides it for long, and the clues are easy to spot once you know them. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Any smell of gas around the appliance, which calls for leaving and phoning the gas utility
  • Less hot water than the tank should deliver
  • A flame that looks orange or floppy rather than steady and blue
  • Exhaust odor, rust, or moisture around the draft hood and vent pipe
  • No hot water at all, with the burner never firing
  • Water pooling around the base of the heater
  • A tank that pops and bangs while it heats, a classic sign of sediment
  • A pilot that lights, then goes out when you release the knob

The Benefits of Professional Gas Water Heater Services

Consider the risks first. Fuel piping, an open flame, and exhaust laced with carbon monoxide come with every gas water heater. A loose flare or threaded joint can leak gas, a vent that is disconnected or poorly sloped can spill exhaust back into the room, and a gas valve installed without a leak test is a risk you cannot see. Then there is the diagnostic side: a pilot that goes out can mean a weak thermocouple, a dirty pilot orifice, a clogged flame arrestor, a draft problem, or a failing gas valve, and replacing them one by one is slow and costly.

Tips to Keep Your Water Heater Healthy

A little routine care goes a long way toward keeping a water heater running well past its expected lifespan. Start with these practical measures our team relies on:

  • Replace a spent anode rod before the tank itself starts to rust
  • Flush the tank on a regular schedule so sediment never bakes onto the bottom
  • Clean dust from around the base so the burner gets plenty of combustion air
  • Check that the vent connector is secure and rising toward the chimney or flue
  • Keep the thermostat around 120 degrees to save energy and reduce scalding risk
  • Never store flammable liquids near a gas water heater
  • Keep working CO alarms near bedrooms and on each floor
