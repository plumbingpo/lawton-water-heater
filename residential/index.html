No Surprises

Honest, Upfront Pricing

Nobody likes a surprise on the invoice. So Lawton Water Heater prices every job upfront, after a full diagnostic that covers the heater's age, fuel type, and capacity, the burner assembly or heating elements, the anode rod and relief valve, and the vent and gas connections. You'll know what's wrong, what it takes to fix, and exactly what it will cost before you say yes.

The trouble with water heater failures is that many of them develop out of sight. Minerals build a crust on the tank floor, the anode rod wears down gradually, and when it's gone, the tank itself starts to corrode. If the water turns rusty, the tank rumbles, or you spot moisture under the heater, get it looked at while it's still a simple repair.

Not every call is an emergency. Many homeowners call us to evaluate an aging heater before it fails, and it's a smart move. With a typical tank lifespan of 8 to 12 years (tankless units often run 20 years with annual descaling), a replacement on your own timeline beats scrambling for a new heater with water on the floor. We'll give you a straight answer on how much life your equipment has left.

Preventive Care

Tank Flushing and Tune-Ups That Pay Off

The best repair is the one you never need. That's the idea behind our annual maintenance. Once a year, we drain and flush the tank, check the anode rod, exercise the relief valve, inspect the venting and gas connections or the electrical components, and run a descaling cycle on tankless units, all tailored to the heater in your home.

We offer flexible maintenance options for homeowners, with annual service for most tanks and twice-yearly flushing where the water is hard. That's especially important where mineral content is high, where sediment insulates the tank bottom, makes the burner work harder, and shortens the life of the tank. Each home is assessed individually, and the plan is tailored to your equipment's age, your household's hot water needs, and your local water.

The savings show up faster than most people expect. A tank without a sediment crust transfers heat with less effort, a fresh anode rod keeps corrosion off the tank walls, and a tested relief valve is ready to do its job if pressure ever climbs too high. Compared with the cost of an early replacement, regular maintenance is a bargain.

Proper Sizing

Hot Water Sized to Your Family's Demand

An undersized water heater runs out halfway through the second shower, while an oversized one burns fuel keeping gallons you never use hot around the clock. Lawton Water Heater sizes every replacement and new installation to your actual household. For tank models, that means looking at the first-hour rating to your busiest hour, rather than simply copying the gallon size on the old tank. For tankless models, it means sizing the flow rate in GPM against the fixtures you run at once and how cold your incoming water gets in winter.

Picking a new water heater is about more than the number of gallons. Our technician will compare tank models with tankless units, and gas with electric or heat pump, walking through operating costs, venting and gas line work, electrical service, and the installation itself. Tankless is a strong fit for many families, but it often needs a larger gas line and new venting, and a well-sized tank can be the simpler, lower-cost option. The decision is yours, based on your home and budget.

Frequent Failures

Hot Water Problems We Solve Every Week

No hot water, a burner that refuses to light, a rumbling, sediment-heavy tank, a slow drip from the relief valve: every one of these problems has a different cause and calls for a different repair. Lawton Water Heater tracks down the exact fault before replacing anything, whether it's an electric tank that trips its breaker, a thermocouple that no longer holds the pilot, a broken dip tube sending cold water straight to the tap, or a tankless unit flashing error codes.

Some failures can wait a day, and some can't. A bad element only cuts your hot water supply. A relief valve spitting water, on the other hand, is a safety device doing its job and telling you something is wrong, and water pooling under the tank can soak into subfloors and drywall before anyone notices. If you smell gas, get everyone outside and call the gas utility first. We come prepared to replace elements, thermostats, thermocouples, gas valves, and relief valves, add a thermal expansion tank when pressure is the culprit, and handle leak repair at the drain valve or fittings.
