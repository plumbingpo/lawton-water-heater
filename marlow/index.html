How Marlow Homeowners Can Extend Water Heater Life

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is simple maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, test the relief valve annually to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which limits scalding risk and cuts down on scale. Tankless owners should plan on descaling once a year in hard-water areas.

How old is your water heater? The serial number on the label usually encodes the month and year it was built, and that date says a lot about what comes next. Most tank models make it to 8 to 12 years, heat pump units 10 to 15, and well-maintained tankless units around 20. If your heater is beyond that range and also shows rusty water, popping sounds, or moisture at the base, it's a strong candidate for a planned replacement, which is almost always cheaper and easier on everyone than an emergency replacement once the tank fails.

Minor issues don't stay minor for long. Take a spent anode rod: swapping it out is a short, inexpensive repair, but if nobody replaces it, the water starts eating the tank itself, and suddenly a minor fix becomes a full replacement. That's why we ask homeowners to keep our number in their phone and call at the first hint of trouble. Catching it early saves time and money and spares you a flooded utility room.

What Goes Wrong With Local Water Heaters

Long before a tank floods a Marlow garage, it almost always shows warning signs. Watch for a popping or rumbling sound during heating, rust-colored hot water, a sulfur smell from the hot tap, moisture around the base, or hot water that runs out a little sooner each month. Those point to sediment buildup, a spent anode rod, a failing element, or corrosion starting at the bottom seam, and caught early, most of them mean a modest repair or a replacement on your schedule. Left alone, they lead to a ruptured tank and a cleanup bill. Pay close attention to a relief valve that drips or discharges: it's the tank telling you pressure or temperature is running too high, and it should be checked promptly. We diagnose the cause, not just the symptom, so small problems stay small.
