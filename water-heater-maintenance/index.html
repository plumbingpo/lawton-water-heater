Here is what shortens most water heaters' lives: corrosion from the inside and mineral buildup on the bottom, both of which are largely preventable. As water heats, dissolved minerals drop out of solution and settle into a layer of sediment on the tank floor. On a gas unit, that layer insulates the water from the burner, so the steel runs hotter and the tank starts to rumble and pop. On an electric unit, it buries the lower element until the element burns out. Meanwhile the anode rod, a metal rod designed to corrode so the tank does not, is slowly used up. Once it is gone, the tank itself becomes the target. In Lawton, OK, we see this pattern constantly, and most of it was preventable.

We work through a full checklist every time. The tank is drained and flushed to clear the sediment layer. The anode rod comes out for inspection and is replaced when little of it remains. The T&P relief valve is tested so we know it will open under pressure and close again cleanly. On gas models, we check the burner, pilot assembly, thermocouple or flame sensor, flue, and draft. Electric heaters get element and thermostat testing and a check of the wiring connections. We also measure the air charge in the expansion tank, check the water pressure, set the thermostat to a scald-safe level unless a mixing valve allows higher storage, and inspect the drain valve, connections, and pan.

If you have a tankless unit, the routine changes but the goal is the same. Minerals coat the heat exchanger over time, reducing efficiency and eventually causing shutdowns. Our technicians connect a circulation pump to the isolation valves and run a descaling solution through the exchanger, clean the water filter, inspect the venting, and check the condensate drain on condensing models. When to Call for Water Heater Maintenance

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you money and keep a slow drip from turning into a flooded utility room. If any of the following sound familiar, it's worth having the unit checked:

  • Higher gas or electric costs for the same routine
  • Temperature swings or reduced flow from a tankless heater
  • A smell like rotten eggs that shows up only on the hot side
  • Rusty or discolored hot water at the tap
  • More than a year since anyone flushed the tank
  • A tank that pops and knocks during a heating cycle
  • Water in the drain pan or on the floor near the heater
  • Hot water running out sooner than it used to

The Benefits of Professional Water Heater Maintenance

There is a short list of things you can do yourself: watching for leaks, keeping the area around the heater clear, and noting new noises. Beyond that, the work gets riskier. Draining a tank means handling scalding water and a drain valve that may not close again if it is old and full of sediment. Pulling an anode rod often takes a breaker bar and real leverage on a fitting that has not moved in years. Testing a T&P valve on an older heater can leave it dripping afterward. On gas units, the burner, thermocouple, and venting involve combustion safety, and a vent that is not drafting can put carbon monoxide into the house.

Simple Ways to Extend Your Water Heater's Life

A little routine care goes a long way toward keeping a water heater running well past its expected lifespan. Here's where to begin:

  • Leave open space around the tank, especially near a gas burner
  • Look around the base of the heater periodically for moisture
  • Treat hard water to cut down on sediment and scale
  • Have the anode rod checked every few years and replaced when it is mostly gone
  • Set the water temperature around 120 degrees for safety and efficiency
  • Schedule a flush every year, or every six months if you have hard water
  • Stay on top of tankless descaling to avoid error codes and reduced flow
