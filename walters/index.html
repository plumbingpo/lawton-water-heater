Keeping Your Walters Water Heater Running Longer

Most early failures trace back to skipped maintenance, and the habits that prevent them are easy. Flush the tank once a year to clear sediment, get the anode rod checked every few years, and operate the relief valve annually so it doesn't seize. Keep the temperature near 120 degrees to reduce scald risk and slow scale buildup. If you have a tankless unit, descale it yearly where the water is hard to protect the heat exchanger.

How old is your water heater? The rating plate on the tank usually includes the month and year it was built, and that date tells you a lot. Most tank models reach 8 to 12 years, heat pump models 10 to 15, and regularly descaled tankless units around 20. If your heater is beyond that range and also shows discolored water, popping sounds, or dampness under the tank, it's usually due for a planned replacement, which is almost always cheaper and easier on everyone than an emergency replacement after a leak.

The sooner you call about a hot water problem, the smaller the repair tends to be. A sediment layer that's just starting to rumble can often be fixed with a flush, yet the same sediment, ignored for a few years, can create hot spots on the tank floor and burn out the lower element on an electric model, turning a simple visit into a replacement. Save our number in your contacts today so help is one call away the moment something seems off. A quick call saves time, money, and cold showers.

What Goes Wrong With Local Water Heaters

Every type of water heater fails in its own way, and we work on all of them in Walters. Gas tanks tend to have trouble with pilot assemblies and thermocouples, dirty burners, and venting that doesn't draft. Electric units burn out heating elements and thermostats, especially when sediment buries the lower element. Tankless heaters show error codes for scale in the heat exchanger, ignition faults, and flow sensor problems, most of which trace back to skipped descaling. Hybrid heat pump units depend on a clean filter and a free-flowing condensate line, and they don't do well in small, closed-in spaces. We service all four types regularly and stock the common parts to finish most repairs in one trip.
